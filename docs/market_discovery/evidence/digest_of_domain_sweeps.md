## insurance-claims  (full file: clusters/insurance-claims.json)

**Finder assessment.** Evidence quality for the cluster is moderate overall, and the direction is mixed-to-negative for a standalone product. Method caveat: 27 WebSearch queries were run before the session budget was exhausted; ~40 fetch attempts across regulator, ombudsman, court, industry-association, government and vendor domains were all blocked by the egress proxy except raw.githubusercontent.com, so every number is from search snippets (URL and source type recorded) and could not be verified against the primary document.

What the evidence supports: (1) Recurring classes of coverage ambiguity genuinely exist and are named by authorities — wear-and-tear/maintenance exclusions (CGC: over half of IDR overturns), non-damage BI trigger wordings (FCA v Arch: ~700 wordings, 370,000 policyholders), 'accident'/'invalidité' definitions and 'exclusions floues' (Médiation de l'Assurance), CI condition definitions (ABI). (2) A settlement loop exists and is even regulator-mandated: DISP 1.3.3 root-cause analysis, FCA/FOS Wider Implications Framework, LMA recommendations followed >99% per case, CMS-4201-F public coverage criteria with annual UM-committee review, Unum's regulator-imposed claims standards. (3) Institutional owners exist inside insurers (authority matrices, Technical Claims Leads, Lloyd's claims leads, medical directors) and a commercial precedent corpus already sells (FC&S 'Ask the Experts').

What the evidence says against: In the best-quantified workflow (US health), interpretive denials are 5-6% of denials versus ~60% administrative/other/excluded (H1), and interpretations are already captured as versioned, published, regulator-governed medical policies (H3), with clinicians legally required in the loop (H4). In P&C, motor coverage is ~99% accepted so disputes are valuation/negotiation (F/B); STP <10% points to evidence and valuation bottlenecks; and the prospective settlements that matter were made by courts, regulators, ombudsmen and industry bodies, then encoded into manuals, wordings and training — the insurer is a rule-taker more than a rule-settler. The CGC's repeated wear-and-tear overturns are the single best sign that insurers fail to apply a settled class prospectively, but that failure is at least as plausibly incentive-driven as knowledge-driven, and an internal precedent ledger does not fix incentives. No source quantified the share of claims escalated for a coverage question, its recurrence within one insurer, or the cost per referral — the parameters that decide the hypothesis remain unmeasured.

Recommendation for this cluster: health claims — negative. Life/disability — weak (low volume, medical classification dominates, discretion retained). P&C coverage determination and the complaints/RCA loop — uncertain, and the only places worth a base-rate experiment. The cheapest experiment is not inside an insurer: classify 300-500 published FOS (or AFCA/LMA) insurance decisions and, ideally, one insurer's technical-referral log, into the brief's eight categories to measure how often the referred ambiguity is (7) previously settled or (8) settleable prospectively; the CGC wear-and-tear result predicts a non-trivial share of (7), which would be the first real support for the thesis in this domain. Interviews should target Technical Claims Leads / Heads of Technical Claims at UK home insurers and Lloyd's syndicates, and ask for the referral log, the QA closed-file review categories, and how a referral outcome is written back into guidelines or ClaimCenter rules. Killing answers: referrals are <2% of claims; outcomes are already written into the claims manual/rules within a release cycle; or referrals are dominated by valuation and evidence rather than wording.

**Verifier overall.** The finder's cluster picture is directionally right but overstated in two places. Its 'uncertain' verdicts for P&C coverage determination and for the complaints/RCA loop should both be 'weak': the numbers behind them are snippets from blocked primaries or vendor blogs, the H shares (5-10%) have no measurement basis and were built by applying disputed-tail issue categories to a human-review pool that is overwhelmingly process, evidence and valuation work, and every prospective settlement the finder found was made by a court, regulator, ombudsman or industry body and then encoded as wording, criteria documents, manuals or training (H3 in external form), with 'fair and reasonable' ombudsman review and legal estoppel risk keeping insurer-internal precedent non-authoritative (H4). The flagship P&C example (CGC wear-and-tear overturns) is a category error: the recurring work is per-property causation evidence and second expert reports (A/B), and the class meaning is already externally settled. The health 'negative' is confirmed and strengthened by the one primary this verifier could actually read, the AHIP June 2025 survey (industry association): only ~7% of medical claims face prior authorisation, ~97-98% of those are approved, initial denials are attributed to incomplete information, and 100% of responding plans keep clinicians on medical-necessity denials; regulation (CMS-4201-F, CMS 2024 FAQ, California SB 1120) locks that in. The finder's proposed FOS-decision experiment cannot deliver the brief's base rate because it samples only disputes; the only cheap test that answers the actual question is one insurer's technical-referral log, and nothing found by finder or verifier predicts a favourable answer. Corrected cluster assessment: negative for health, weak for P&C, life/disability and the complaints loop; no sourced number in the cluster supports the core hypothesis that insurers repeatedly pay humans to settle the same class of ambiguity for lack of a capture mechanism. Verification caveat: zero web-search budget and total proxy denial meant re-checks were limited to one cached primary, internal consistency and labelled recall; no finder number was newly verified against its primary, and none was newly refuted on its face, only on how it was used.

## insurance-underwriting  (full file: clusters/insurance-underwriting.json)

**Finder assessment.** Insurance underwriting is WEAK evidence for a standalone Sophon-shaped product, with one structurally interesting but unmeasured pocket. The settlement loop the brief describes demonstrably exists in this domain: reinsurer impairment manuals and AUW rule tables (life), appetite guides and referral rules (commercial), and binding-authority terms with mandatory referral of out-of-binder risks (delegated authority) are all versioned instruments through which an owner's decision is applied prospectively. But three things cut against the product. First, the residual human work is mostly not category H: in life, 41% of applications are on a traditional path because of bright-line age/amount limits (Gen Re 2024) and kick-outs are evidence-driven (SOA taxonomy); in commercial, the measured waste is data extraction (30–60 min/submission; 30–40% of underwriter time per McKinsey); referrals are predominantly authority gates on capacity and price (E/F). Second, where recurring referral classes exist they are already codified quickly inside incumbent tooling: one MGA wrote >30 Federato rules that automate 95% of referrals; rules-engine vendors replay submissions, report referrals by trigger and tune tables; reinsurers own and version the life manual and subsidise the engine. This supports H3 and H6 — a settlement ledger would be a feature of the workbench or rules engine that already holds the rule. Third, judgment is explicitly protected and governed: carriers reserve 'individual consideration', out-of-binder risks legally require managing-agent agreement every time, and Colorado SB21-169, NY DFS CL7 and the NAIC AI bulletin make every outcome-changing rule a tested, documented model change (H4). Personal lines and health are negative (95% STP; no data). The one pocket worth a customer-discovery probe is delegated authority special acceptances at Lloyd's/MGA scale (>4,000 coverholders, ~39% of Lloyd's GWP, explicit referral rule, annual binder as the settlement instrument), because there the owner, the escalation rule and the prospective instrument all exist and nobody has published referral volumes or recurrence — but that is an absence of data, not evidence for the product. Confidence is limited: the egress proxy blocked every primary reinsurer, regulator, association and vendor page, so all figures rest on search snippets and one fetched vendor/press page; no exception rate, override rate or cost per referral was found anywhere.

**Verifier overall.** Verification was severely constrained: the session's WebSearch budget was exhausted before this verifier ran, and every primary source the finder cited was egress-blocked for both WebFetch and curl, so no load-bearing number could be re-fetched. Within those limits the finder's work is directionally honest but systematically over-generous on two axes. First, it presents vendor blog assertions as operational rates (the 35–50% commercial referral rate rests on one rules-engine vendor's unsupported blog; the '50–90% human involvement' in life is a synthesis of two incompatible denominators, programme eligibility from Gen Re versus point-of-sale STP from Swiss Re) and cites an unsourced '48+ states' NAIC adoption figure that looks wrong. Second, its settlement-loop evidence is almost entirely vendor-authored descriptions of rule-tuning features, which is evidence for H3/H6 (incumbents already own the loop), not for the loop being an unmet need; the life 'settlement' (reinsurer impairment manual) is rule authoring by an external actuarial owner, not case-to-precedent settlement; the DA 'settlement' (special acceptance) is per-risk by construction and becomes prospective only via a binder endorsement that already exists as a versioned, legally required instrument. Re-estimated H shares are 1–3% of reviewed life cases, 3–5% of commercial referrals and 5–10% of DA referrals (each a small minority of decisions), all estimates with no measurement available. What I could fetch (an underwriting-workbench vendor list, a Cytora/Google Cloud piece, two portfolio models of commercial referral logic and Lloyd's DA oversight, an AWS life-underwriting demo, the Prudential dataset) consistently shows referral triggers are authority gates and classification (B/E/F), oversight pain is operational, vendors locate the waste in evidence gathering (A), and no public corpus carries referral reasons or settlement outcomes. Corrected verdicts: life negative (was weak), SMB commercial weak (was uncertain), delegated authority weak (was uncertain, remains the one cheap interview probe), personal lines negative, health negative. The cluster as a whole is negative-to-weak evidence for a standalone Sophon-shaped product: the loop the brief describes exists in underwriting but is executed through instruments that are already versioned and owned (manuals, appetite rules, binders), humans stay in the loop by treaty, market practice or regulation, and the addressable labour pool (on the order of 100k US underwriters across all lines, most of whose time is data handling) is too small at the 1–5% H share to support standalone economics.

## banking-lending-exceptions  (full file: clusters/banking-lending-exceptions.json)

**Finder assessment.** NETWORK CAVEAT FIRST: this finder could not verify a single primary source. WebSearch returned "budget exhausted (200/200)" on the very first query; WebFetch and curl were refused by the egress proxy for occ.gov, fdic.gov, federalreserve.gov, ffiec.gov, consumerfinance.gov, fanniemae.com (all subdomains), freddiemac.com, fhfaoig.gov, hud.gov, eba.europa.eu, eur-lex.europa.eu, acpr.banque-france.fr, legifrance.gouv.fr, bis.org, ecfr.gov, govinfo.gov, federalregister.gov, sec.gov, ncino.com, moodys.com, fico.com, icemortgagetechnology.com, acesquality.com, mba.org, urban.org, wikipedia, web.archive.org and ~30 other domains; the GitHub search API is repo-scoped in this session and returned 503. Only raw.githubusercontent.com, PyPI and npm were reachable, which yielded three marginal READMEs (Catala, CFPB HMDA platform, an agency loan-level data loader). Everything below that is not from those three files is MODEL RECALL, flagged as such, and must be re-verified before anyone acts on it. Evidence quality is therefore "unknown" or "weak" across the board by construction, not because the domain was shown to be empty.

Substantive picture from recall, to be tested: (1) US bank supervisors (OCC Loan Portfolio Management handbook, FDIC RMS Manual s.3.2, 2020 Interagency Guidance on Credit Risk Review Systems) require that exceptions to loan policy be approved at a designated authority level, documented, aggregated and reported to the board, and they treat a rising exception rate as a signal that either underwriting is loosening or the policy needs revision. That is a real, regulator-mandated loop from repeated exceptions to policy change, but the exceptions themselves are overwhelmingly (a) missing-document exceptions (category A) or (b) per-loan discretionary deviations from bright-line limits such as LTV, DSCR, amortisation, guarantor support (categories E/F/G). The settlement is "this loan, this time", not a prospective reading. Vendors (nCino policy-exception object, Abrigo/Sageworks exception tracking, Baker Hill, Moody's CreditLens, spreadsheets) capture the per-loan exception and its approver for reporting, and none, to my knowledge, turn a decision into a versioned reusable rule; but the reason is that the bank does not want them reused: an exception that becomes a rule is by definition a policy amendment, which the Chief Credit Officer / credit policy committee already handles at policy-review cadence. This looks like the IPSA pattern again: plentiful repetition where the owner already governs with bright lines, discretion where disputes arise. (2) The one genuinely prospective, high-volume settlement loop in the cluster is the GSE interpretation loop: lender question (Ask Poli / account team / Freddie Mac Seller support) -> GSE clarification -> Selling Guide / Guide Bulletin update that binds all sellers. That loop exists, recurs, is versioned and is executable in DU/LPA. But the owner is two institutions (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) plus FHA/VA/USDA, and the downstream lender-side artefact ("overlays", "scenario desk" answers) is already encoded in LOS business rules (Encompass), investor eligibility engines and product/pricing engines. This is H3 (already encoded adequately) and H8 (owner pool too small) territory unless the lender-side scenario-desk knowledge proves to be badly captured, which I could not verify. (3) In the EU/France, EBA GL/2020/06 on loan origination requires documented, escalated and monitored deviations from credit policy and permits automated decisioning with governance; EU AI Act Annex III 5(b) makes creditworthiness assessment / credit scoring of natural persons high-risk (human oversight, logging, right to explanation), and CJEU SCHUFA (C-634/21) treats credit scoring as an Art. 22 GDPR automated decision. France additionally runs the clearest quota-governed exception regime I know of: the HCSF binding decision lets banks exceed the 35% DSTI / 25-year maturity limits for up to 20% of quarterly mortgage production, with sub-quotas, and ACPR/HCSF publish the utilisation rate. That is a regulator-designed exception budget, and it argues AGAINST the thesis: the ambiguity is not interpretive, it is a rationed discretion pool. Net: the cluster contains real, audited exception-tracking work and a real GSE-owned settlement loop, but the per-decision interpretive (category H) share on the lender side is probably small, incumbents already hold the per-loan record, and the prospective loop is owned by a handful of guide publishers. I would rate the cluster "uncertain leaning weak" pending the verification this session could not perform; the single thing worth testing empirically is a mortgage lender's scenario-desk / underwriting-help-desk log, to measure how many questions are repeats of a previously answered guideline interpretation versus missing-document or discretionary questions.

**Verifier overall.** The finder's honesty about its evidence base is warranted and my verification could add only three sourced items (ServiceNow GRC and Loan Operations documentation, the EBA Single Rulebook Q&A corpus, CFPB HMDA platform docs); every regulator, GSE, EU, vendor and press host is egress-blocked and the search budget was exhausted, so all volume and rate figures in the finder result remain unsupported recall. On substance the finder's cluster picture is directionally right but too generous in two places: it counted loan policy exceptions (approved bright-line breaches, category E, dominated by documentation exceptions) and lender overlays (risk-appetite policy) as candidate settlements, and it assigned a 40% H share to GSE guide-publishing events where no per-case review exists. Corrected, the cluster has one genuinely prospective, versioned settlement loop, which is owned by the GSEs/FHA/EBA-style regulators who already publish and execute it (the EBA analogue shows 5 loan-origination Q&As in five years and a 248-day median answer lag, so the loop is real, captured and tiny), while lender-side interpretation of that guide cannot be made authoritative by the lender and is being encoded by the owner (income calculators with rep-and-warrant relief). The lender-side underwriting help-desk log is still the only thing worth sampling, but the prior should be that most repeats are eligibility lookups, missing documents or priced exceptions rather than settleable ambiguity. ## healthcare-prior-auth-and-claims  (full file: clusters/healthcare-prior-auth-and-claims.json)

**Finder assessment.** Overall: weak evidence for a standalone Sophon-shaped product in healthcare prior auth and claims, with one moderate pocket. The cluster has the volume (53M MA PA decisions/year, 15% initial claim denial rates, $25.7B provider adjudication cost) and the cost per decision ($3-11 per PA, $57 per denied claim, $75+ per physician review), but the human labour is dominated by missing evidence and criteria matching (AHIP: denials 'often a result of incomplete information'; 26.6% of claim denials are registration/eligibility; 80-100% overturn rates once records arrive), and the interpretive residue is legally reserved to physicians (SB 1120, AHIP pledge, CMS rules) so precedent cannot displace the reviewer on denials. The settlement loop the brief asks for genuinely exists here, but it is already institutionalised and versioned: medical policy committees and technology assessment committees produce numbered, effective-dated policies; MCG/InterQual editions carry plan customisations; CMS-4201-F forces internal coverage criteria into public, annually reviewed documents; AHA Coding Clinic and LCD reconsideration settle industry-level ambiguity prospectively; Da Vinci DTR encodes payer rules as executable CQL. That is direct support for H3 and H4, and the settlement volume in the formal loops is tiny relative to decision volume (support for H2). The most Sophon-like pocket is recurring payer-provider interpretive disputes in claims (sepsis and other clinical-validation definitions, E/M leveling, modifiers, inpatient status) that get settled bilaterally by contract or policy update and recur across thousands of claims; a provider-side 'payer interpretation playbook' with provenance is plausible but evidence for the gap is anecdotal and incumbents (Cotiviti, ClaimsXten, Epic, Waystar, outsourced denial firms) are strong. France is negative (deemed approval by silence, single public buyer, ambiguity settled by HAS référentiels). Caveats: the egress proxy blocked nearly all primary pages (KFF, OIG, CMS, CAQH, AMA, Premier, eCFR), so most numbers rest on search snippets of those sources; only the AHIP report and the HL7 Da Vinci GitHub pages were fetched in full; the WebSearch budget was exhausted before confirming Texas gold-card uptake, Coding Clinic inquiry volumes and French DAP volumes. Recommendation for the parent: do not pick this cluster as the wedge; if any experiment is run here, sample ~200 hospital denial-appeal cases at one health system and classify whether the winning argument was new evidence (A), criteria match (B) or a reusable payer-specific interpretation (H) - the prior evidence suggests H will be under 10%.

**Verifier overall.** The finder's conclusion (weak cluster, do not pick as wedge) survives verification and should be strengthened, not softened. The one load-bearing source I could read in full (AHIP June 2025) checks out exactly: 7% of commercial medical claims subject to PA, 97-98% approved, ~20% real-time, 45% manual, and an explicit, already-in-effect commitment that every medical-necessity denial is clinician-reviewed. ## government-benefits-and-casework  (full file: clusters/government-benefits-and-casework.json)

**Finder assessment.** Negative-to-weak for a standalone Sophon product in government benefits and casework, with one uncertain sub-case (US unemployment insurance) and one interesting-but-unbuyable pattern (French MDPH disparities). The settlement loop the brief asks for demonstrably exists in every jurisdiction examined, and that is precisely the problem: it is already institutionalised in law and owned by the authority's legal/policy unit — SSA Acquiescence Rulings and SSRs written into POMS/HALLEX within 120 days of a circuit decision; VA General Counsel precedent opinions binding on all VA employees until superseded; California CUIAB precedent decisions designated for issues 'of general application likely to recur' and binding on EDD; DWP ADM/DMG memos implementing binding Upper Tribunal decisions; USCIS Policy Manual alerts; URSSAF rescrits de portée générale published in the BOSS. These are versioned, prospective, provenance-carrying interpretations produced at low frequency (single digits to tens per year), triggered mostly by courts rather than frontline escalation, and consumed through manuals that the agencies already maintain. Meanwhile the mass of human review sits in categories A, B and E: Medicaid FY2025 improper payments are 77% insufficient documentation; SNAP's 10.93% error rate is calculation and failure to act on information; UK PIP overturns are 63% oral evidence and 23% same-facts evaluative disagreement with ~1% new written evidence; SSA ALJ allowance rates span 9% to 93% despite the precedent apparatus; CAF's €6.3bn of uncorrected error is declarative-data driven. Buyers are single national agencies or dozens of state/departmental bodies with slow procurement and legal limits on automated adverse decisions (GDPR Art 22, DPA 2018, CRPA L311-3-1, APA de novo hearing rights), and formula-level codification is already covered by rules-as-code (OpenFisca, Catala). The only Sophon-shaped gap not closed by evidence is whether frontline adjudicators fail to apply already-published precedents consistently (UI nonmonetary determinations, MDPH local doctrine across 100+ commissions) — that was not established because volume and reversal data were blocked, and even if true the owner is fragmented or is the government itself. I would not pursue this cluster as an initial wedge; if the owner wants one test here, it is a base-rate classification of CUIAB precedent-decision citations in EDD determinations or of UT-decision citations in DWP MR outcomes, which requires agency access rather than public data.

Category split across the cluster (analytical): front-line escalation in the only inspected production system is coded as missing evidence (REQUEST_SAMPLE/REQUEST_MORE_INFO), and DWP's tooling investment is in evidence checks - supporting H1 for operational workflows. H (repeatable ambiguity settled prospectively) is clearly present but is handled by institutions as a rule-making function, not a case-handling function, and they ration it deliberately (fees, non-precedent clauses, expiry-and-renew).

Demand signals against a standalone product: PubLedge, an open-source typed/versioned interpretation register covering PLRs, no-action letters, advisory opinions and sandbox agreements, found no adopters and parked its product ambitions in June 2026; Nirmata's register explicitly refuses to turn recurring exceptions into policy; GitHub keeps cases out of policy edits. The strongest Sophon-shaped evidence in this cluster is the private analogue - Big-4 national-office consultation databases and importer classification databases inside GTM suites - both unverified this session and both already built in-house or by incumbents (H3/H6).

Conclusion for this cluster: the settlement loop is a real, named, operational institution mainly where the settled object is a classification that maps to a code (tariff, ICD, commodity) or an executable policy exception; it is ceremonial or deliberately severed wherever the settled object is an interpretation of text. Candidate follow-ups with cheap access to real cases: HMRC/EU customs classification (open-source system, public rulings), AHA Coding Clinic + encoder vendors, and Kyverno/OPA exception registers in large platform teams. I would not pursue regulator-facing interpretation registers (PubLedge signal) or content-policy loops (Oversight Board data).

VERDICTS. H1 (missing facts, not ambiguity): PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, domain-dependent. But in the two appeal/complaint corpora with sourced distributions, missing evidence is a minor primary driver (IPSA: 2 of 46 primary issues; FOS insurance: 10 of 626 upheld = 1.6%) while 16-20 of 46 IPSA outcomes still turned on facts gathered AFTER the original decision. H1 is therefore right that H is small, but wrong about why: the residual is discretion and handling, which is worse for the product than missing facts.

H2 (ambiguity repeats too rarely to compound): SUPPORTED at the granularity where disputes actually arise, NOT SUPPORTED at coarse class level. IPSA 1,000-claim recurrence: one settlement per class covers 93% at category+cost-type (67 classes, 22% of settlements never reused) but 37% at what-was-bought (633 classes, 86% seen once). H3 (organisations already encode settlements adequately): PARTIALLY SUPPORTED / MIXED. Net: the gap is real but it is a gap in WHO IS EMPOWERED to ratify and HOW FAST, not a gap in encoding tooling - the same conclusion the prior investigation reached.

OVERALL for the falsification cluster: the strongest sourced pattern across three independent corpora (IPSA n=46, FOS n=1,349, IPSA recurrence n=1,000) is that once bright-line rules and after-the-fact audit are in place, the human work that remains is dominated by discretion on unique facts and by handling quality, with rule-meaning disputes at 2.6% (FOS) to 26% (IPSA) and prospective settlement power scarce.
