## A. Executive answer

Evidence against a standalone Sophon product, on the verified record, with one defined and cheap route to falsify that call.

Why, in four parts that hold across all fifteen clusters.

1. No sourced number anywhere supports the core hypothesis that organisations repeatedly pay humans to settle the same class of ambiguity for lack of a capture mechanism. Where a why-humans-review distribution was actually sourced, H is small and the residual is not missing facts but discretion, handling and per-item factual classification. FOS insurance: policy-wording interpretation is the ground in 16 of 626 upheld decisions (2.6%) and 1.2% of 1,349 claim decisions across 183 firms (FOS, regex floor, weak). IPSA: of 46 reviews, discretion or exception 18, rule meaning 12, missing evidence 2, and IPSA committed to 4 of 17 recommended rule changes (IPSA). SNAP FY2024 QC error dollars: 77.7% household-side input, 4.6% pure computation (GOV-DATA). SSA rates DDS determinations 97.1% policy-accurate in FY2022 (GOV-DATA). Prior authorisation: 7% of commercial medical claims face PA, 97 to 98% are approved, initial denials are "often a result of incomplete information" (AHIP).

2. Every real prospective settlement loop found is run by the rule owner, not by a software buyer, and is already versioned, published or executable. HMRC ATaR records each ruling with justification, search method, exclusion method, effective dates, typed cancellation reasons and an appeal ladder (ATAR). The EBA publishes 2,166 final Q&As indexed by act, article and paragraph (EBA). Visa CE3.0 is a typed evidence object in Stripe's API (STRIPE). GSE DU and LPA, CMS-4201-F coverage criteria, reinsurer manuals and Lloyd's binder endorsements are the same shape [RECALL].

3. Where the loop lives inside an operating organisation, the incumbent already ships it as an executable prospective primitive. Marble RuleSnooze carries creator, source decision, rule, pivot, start and expiry, and an LLM case reviewer reads prior cases (MARBLE). ServiceNow IRM holds an exception record with requester, approver, validity dates, sys_audit, retained retired document versions and case-to-knowledge (SNOW). Avalara holds effective-dated, user-stamped tax rules, typed dispute and reclassification flags, a versioned company classification mode, a write-back webhook on HSCodeAssigned and a managed classification SLA (AVALARA). Elastic creates exceptions from an alert; Claude Code writes an allow rule from a prompt (vendor docs, fetched). The residual gap is narrow: no inspected incumbent records "settled by X on date D under power P as rule R version V applied prospectively". The incumbents most likely to hold that object (IBM ODM Decision Center, Pega, Drools Business Central) could not be documented, so even the gap is unproven.

4. Wherever the settled object is an interpretation of text rather than a code, the institution keeps individual settlements non-precedential on purpose: IRC 6110(k)(3) quoted in every PLR (28,135-file primary corpus), IPSA Scheme s.2.3 (IPSA), a clinician on every medical-necessity denial (AHIP), FOS fair-and-reasonable, DSA Art. 21(2), an ERISA named fiduciary on every appeal [RECALL for the last three]. The one open-source product built for exactly this artefact class has 0 stars and parked its product ambitions (PUBLEDGE), a weak but consistent demand signal.

What would overturn the call. Four company-side review pools were never observed because their data is not public: an importer's tariff classification log, a PSP's merchant-underwriting queue, a bank's CDD exception log and a mortgage lender's help-desk log. Every verifier named one of these as the only thing still worth testing and set the prior at a single-digit to low-teens H share. The three deep dives on those pools, after refutation, each returned evidence against. Section H specifies the one test worth running.

Geography. The direction holds in the US, the EU and France. The EU and France add human-oversight floors on adverse consumer decisions (GDPR Art. 22, AI Act Annex III) [RECALL] that the US lacks. France adds regulator-designed discretion budgets (the HCSF mortgage margin) [RECALL], which are rationed discretion, not ambiguity.

## C. Top three workflows

Each is carried as "evidence against" after refutation. They are the three where a company log could in principle be coded.

### C1. In-company customs tariff classification

Triggering event. A new SKU or part enters the item master, sourcing moves to a new country, a supplier changes composition, an annual HTS or CN edition renumbers a line, or a broker, port officer or audit challenges a declared code. Avalara models the state machine: bulk classification jobs with counters classified, inProgress, notClassified, failed and notFound, a TariffCodeNeedsReview criterion, and a request object with flags isReclassification, isDisputed, isPriority, isExport, isExportControl and isCompliance (AVALARA). The regulator-side analogue of a dispute is HMRC's LiabilityOrder with traderCommodityCode versus officerCommodityCode (ATAR).

Available rules. HS nomenclature with six General Interpretative Rules and binding section and chapter notes; national extensions (HTSUS 10-digit, CN 8-digit, TARIC 10-digit); explanatory notes; published rulings (CROSS 216,164; UK ATaR public search refreshed daily; EU EBTI blocked). Legal effect differs by geography and is recall: a US ruling binds only the holder (19 CFR 177) [RECALL]; an EU BTI binds all Member State authorities and the holder for three years (UCC Art. 33) [RECALL]; France issues RTC through DGDDI under the EU regime [RECALL]; a UK ATaR is "valid from {0} to {1}" for the holder and third parties are told a similar ruling "can help" (ATAR).

Available facts. Description, composition, function, form, origin. Avalara's ItemModel carries parameters flagged isNeededForClassification, classifications per systemCode and country, created and modified user and date, and a premium free-text justification with user and date (AVALARA). Fact gaps are the routine escalation cause: HMRC's only referral reasons are REQUEST_SAMPLE and REQUEST_MORE_INFO, with a NO_INFO_FROM_TRADER reject reason (ATAR). Chapter 99 trade-remedy outcomes "depend on import circumstances, not product text" (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vladswork23/us-hts-classifier/main/README.md, open-source README).

Why it escalates. Four reasons, only one of them H. Missing facts (A). Per-item factual classification on already-settled lines (B): in 2023-24, 97.5 to 98.6% of CBP rulings sat on a 6-digit line and 88 to 94% on a 10-digit line that already had a ruling, depending on how "prior" is defined; the median 2023-24 ruling sat on a 10-digit line with 39 prior rulings and 68% sat on lines with ten or more (CROSS). Interpretation (C): 30.5% of 2023-24 rulings cite a chapter 99 code (0% in 2017), 19% carry origin in the subject, 4% mention a set or kit (CROSS). Conflict (D): 1.41% of rulings were ever modified or revoked, 99.1% of superseding rulings by HQ (CROSS). Caveat that the refuter established: CROSS is a reliance instrument requested on deeply settled lines, so it cannot estimate the A/B/C/H split of an in-company queue in either direction.

Who resolves it. Analyst, then senior specialist, then the trade compliance director, who can set the company position for a family and instruct brokers; beyond that a licensed broker, counsel, or the authority through a ruling. Only the authority's decision binds, and only for the holder. The one reachable practice guide (a hobby skill for attorneys and brokers, weak: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/onurkafk/customs-trade-law/main/references/human-review-triggers.md) routes GRI 3 disputes, conflicting rulings and recurring or disputed classifications to professional review and a ruling request, which externalises the settlement rather than internalising it.

What exactly gets settled. Three layers. (1) The code for this item in this country, stored with dates and user (AVALARA). (2) A family reading such as "our brackets are parts of heading X": the H-shaped object. Avalara has no rule predicate, authority or applicability field, only itemGroup and category strings plus the premium justification text; ONESOURCE, Descartes, SAP GTS and Oracle GTM are unverified. (3) The regulator's ruling with justification, methodSearch, methodCommercialDenomination, methodExclusion, effective dates, a CancelReason enum (INVALIDATED_CODE_CHANGE and others) and an appeal ladder from ADR to Supreme Court (ATAR).

Prospective. Item level, yes: Avalara's TaxRule is effective-dated and user-stamped (AVALARA). Family level: a team convention with no sourced versioned object. Everyone-binding: 29, 27 and 31 HQ superseding rulings in 2022, 2023 and 2024, with a median lag of 3.7 years from ruling to modification, and 2023-24 HQ output dominated by protests (129 of 416 mention protest) (CROSS). 18% of 2023-24 rulings carry no code at all (origin, carriers, entry) (CROSS), so "outcome is a code" holds for about four fifths of decisions.

Recurrence. Sourced at nomenclature level, unknown in-company. Excluding chapters 98 and 99, 85 six-digit lines cover half of 2023-24 code mentions; 62% of 1,512 distinct 10-digit lines appear once in the two years; same-day clusters of rulings for one family citing the same precedent number 11 groups and 41 rulings (three or more) or 28 groups and 75 rulings (two or more), that is 1 to 2% of 3,971 rulings (CROSS). The multi-country multiplier applies to codes stored, not ambiguities settled, because the six-digit core is shared [RECALL].

Current systems. ERP item master, GTM suites, broker systems, CROSS, EBTI and ATaR lookup, Avalara's Classification MCP server offering managed classification requests (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/avadev/mcp/main/README.md, vendor documentation). LLM classifiers grounded in CROSS reach 73, 60, 45, 38 and 33% at 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 digits on 89 held-out rulings (vladswork23 README, anecdotal).

Minimal Sophon product. A settlement register beside the item master that records, per escalated decision, the facts relied on, the rule reading, the authority cited, the decider and their power, effective dates and an applicability predicate; matches new SKUs on attributes; invalidates on typed triggers; exports the audit trail. It would not classify, extract facts, decide chapter 99 or origin, file rulings or replace the GTM. Honest framing after refutation: the inspected incumbent already ships dispute and reclassification flags, a review-queue disposition endpoint, a versioned company classification mode (AutoFull or AutoPartial with settingsVersion), a write-back webhook on HSCodeAssigned, per-item duty detail by origin and manufacturer including punitive and AD/CVD rates, HS verification batches and a managed classification SLA in hours (AVALARA). The residual is the rationale schema.

Buyer. Head of Global Trade Compliance or Customs Manager at importers with thousands of SKUs across two or more regimes; brokers secondary. For customers of the inspected incumbent the classification labour sits at the vendor under an SLA, not in the buyer's team.

ROI logic. All estimates. 5,000 new SKUs a year, 10% escalated, 2 hours per escalation at 60 USD, H at 5 to 15%, 1.5 hours saved per H case: 2,250 to 6,750 USD a year; at ten times the flow, 22,500 to 67,500 USD. Labour cannot carry a product. The error-cost case (a 10 million USD family across a 5-point differential is 500,000 USD a year) is arithmetic on two unsourced premises, and its inconsistency premise is contradicted by the incumbent's like-item verification batches (AVALARA). Verdict: evidence against; survives only as the cheapest place to run section H.

### C2. Merchant onboarding restricted-business classification at a PSP or acquirer

Triggering event. An application, or a periodic re-review or website-monitoring alert, fails to resolve automatically because the business model is not cleanly inside or outside the firm's restricted list or its MCC is contestable. Stripe's account moves to requirements.disabled_reason under_review or a capability to pending.review; outcomes are rejected.listed, rejected.terms_of_service, rejected.unsupported_business, rejected.other or activation (STRIPE). Adyen's capability verificationStatus goes pending then valid, invalid or rejected, "rejected" meaning Adyen "found reasons to not allow the capability" (ADYEN). Ballerine's case enters Manual review with Approve, Reject or Ask for all re-uploads (BALLERINE).

Available rules. The PSP's own prohibited and restricted list, versioned [RECALL, page blocked]; card-network rules on MCC accuracy, brand-risk registration and MATCH [RECALL]; the 981-code MCC space (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/greggles/mcc-codes/main/mcc_codes.csv, third-party dataset); vendor-encoded rules: Adyen types restricted industries as capabilities such as acceptTransactionInRestrictedIndustries (ADYEN); Ballerine gates documents on MCC (5499 and 5912 cannabis licence, 7800 and 7801 gambling licence, 5941 firearms authorisation) and documents that "you can set a rule on an MCC code to auto-approve, auto-reject, or move to manual review" (BALLERINE). Local law overlays: state-legal cannabis, EU and French gambling licensing (ANJ) [RECALL].

Available facts. business_type (4 values), company.structure (23 values), free-string MCC, product_description "used by Stripe for risk and underwriting purposes", URL, owners (STRIPE). Ballerine's merchant report emits mcc, lineOfBusiness and typed risk indicators with reason, quote, source URL and screenshot, including a PROHIBITED_CONTENT violation id (BALLERINE). Missing licences and inaccessible websites are the routine gap.

Why it escalates. Missing evidence (A) and factual classification of what is sold (B) dominate: of Stripe's 96 requirement error codes, 30 are document defects, 17 URL defects, 23 identity, ownership and tax-id mismatches, 19 format codes, 5 denylists and 3 other; none names a restricted-business category decision (STRIPE). Interpretive residue (C, D, H) is whether a hemp subscription is "CBD" or whether a sweepstakes model is "gambling" under the list although lawful locally. Adyen's VerificationError carries a type enum (invalidInput, dataMissing, pendingStatus, rejected, dataReview), so acceptability rationale is typed by error class, not untyped as the deep dive first claimed (ADYEN).

Who resolves it. L1 underwriter; senior underwriter; head of merchant risk or a risk committee with the MLRO for list changes. Rule-making power exists only for principal-member acquirers and PSPs underwriting on their own licence; payfacs and platforms are underwritten by their PSP or sponsor, which is exactly what the Stripe and Adyen APIs show [structure sourced from the specs; sponsor-bank binding is RECALL]. Card-network brand-risk programmes keep a per-merchant registration and due-diligence step for the very verticals where ambiguity sits [RECALL].

What exactly gets settled. A class-level outcome: "models with features X and Y are list category Z, acceptable with conditions, from date D". The executable payload already exists as a list edit, an MCC document rule, a routing rule and a capability flag (BALLERINE, ADYEN). Because the artefact is a prohibited-and-restricted list, the versioned executable part is predominantly the decline or condition side; approvals need no list entry. What is not visibly captured is provenance: source case, reasoning, authority, date, supersession. Ballerine already versions its Policy and WorkflowDefinition objects and stores risk rules as a Domain, Indicator, Risk-level table (BALLERINE); the gap is author, date, rationale and source-case columns on an existing table.

Prospective. Yes by construction for list entries and MCC rules. Caveats: declines of sole traders in the EU and France fall under GDPR Art. 22 [RECALL]; a settlement that conflicts with a network programme is not the PSP's to make; conditional approvals carry merchant-specific reserves and caps (F) that do not generalise.

Recurrence. Unknown. After a class is settled, the next application of that class is a class-membership question (B), as the cluster verifier said. Recurrence of the interpretive question itself is the quantity that matters and nothing separates it from recurrence of members. Analogy from IPSA: one settlement covers 93% at coarse class and 37% at fine class (IPSA).

Current systems. In-house onboarding and underwriting tools exposing typed state through APIs (STRIPE, ADYEN); Ballerine, whose README says the open-source repo "is currently undergoing a major rebuild and is not actively supported" (BALLERINE); Middesk, Alloy, Persona, LegitScript, G2, Web Shield [RECALL].

Minimal Sophon product. A typed settlement object captured at the moment a novel-model case is decided, matched against later applications, chained through supersession, and exported as a rule into the onboarding engine. It would not classify websites, collect documents, set reserves or automate sole-trader declines in the EU. A feature-sized surface.

Buyer. Head of merchant risk at a self-underwriting PSP or acquirer, with the MLRO second. The buyer set is a subset of the recalled low hundreds, and its largest members build in-house.

ROI logic. Estimates only; corrected by the refuter. At a 100,000-application PSP with 15% manual review at 17 USD and 10% escalation at 75 USD, the H pool of 150 to 300 cases yields 9,000 to 16,500 USD of avoidable re-escalation; the L1-automation item in the original deep dive depended on website classification the product disclaims or on MCC rules Ballerine already ships, so gross labour value is about 10,000 to 30,000 USD a year (estimate). That supports a 5,000 to 15,000 USD ACV and an ARR ceiling in the low single-digit millions (estimate). H8 fails outright. Verdict: evidence against; least-bad shape in the payments cluster, empty data base.

ROI logic. Estimates only. 10,000 loans, 0.5 questions per loan, 15 minutes at 75 USD: 93,750 USD a year, about 0.6 FTE. H at 3 to 7%: 2,800 to 6,600 USD; at a generous 20%: 18,750 USD. The whole desk is sub-FTE at a mid-size lender, so no H share rescues standalone pricing. Repurchase risk is driven by income and documentation defects the owner is encoding itself [RECALL]. Access is not cheap: scenario emails carry borrower nonpublic personal information under GLBA and Regulation P [RECALL]. Verdict: evidence against; it holds a top-three slot only by elimination.

## D. Best initial wedge

The historical classification log at one importer that classifies in-house, in the US first (HTSUS, CROSS), with one UK or EU importer among the discovery targets (ATaR or BTI; France as RTC). Not a market-size argument: it is the cheapest place to get a real answer, and the honest prior is that the answer kills it.

Why this workflow. (1) The historical corpus already exists in structured form: Avalara's ItemModel and ItemHSCodeClassificationStatusModel carry item code, description, item group, classifications per country, created and modified user and date, status, details and completedAt (AVALARA); equivalents at ONESOURCE, Descartes, SAP GTS and Oracle GTM are unverified. (2) A public precedent corpus with dates and supersession links is reachable today for the external check (CROSS; ATaR public search). (3) The decision owner holds rule-making power over the company's own filings, though not over the meaning of the heading, which belongs to CBP, HMRC or the EU; this is a partial pass, better than mortgage (GSE-owned), claims (courts and FOS) or reporting (EBA). (4) Decisions are classifications and approvals, not adverse decisions about individuals, so the H4 constraints on denials do not apply. (5) The class key is anchored by a public taxonomy. (6) The outcome is a code plus dates, already executable in the item master, for about four fifths of decisions (CROSS). (7) The incumbent gap is documented, at Avalara only: no rationale on TaxRule, justification only as premium free text, no family rule or applicability object (AVALARA).

Why not the larger markets. Mortgage: the lender cannot make guide readings authoritative, the owners encode the largest interpretive area themselves, the log is email with NPI, and the desk is sub-FTE. Merchant onboarding: in-house tooling, no export pattern examined, PSP access harder than importer access, corrected economics fail H8. KYC/CDD: hundreds to low thousands of exceptions a year (guess), a rationale expected per exception, CLM vendors own the rules. P&C claims: settled object owned externally, wording 2.6% of upheld (FOS, weak). Regulatory reporting: EBA owns the meaning and runs the store free. Lloyd's DA: a single interview, not an experiment.

Access to historical cases. Item-master export; the team's classification worksheet; escalation emails and broker requests for 12 months; broker instruction letters; post-entry corrections, protests and reconciliation adjustments; the classification manual with version history; ruling requests. Brokers hold worksheets for many importers, but those are the importer's confidential records and each log needs that importer's consent (19 CFR 111.24) [RECALL], and a broker earns per-line classification fees [RECALL], so brokers serve as referral and cost-anchor sources, not as the primary log.

Access to decision owners. Director of Global Trade Compliance, Customs Compliance Manager, Classification Lead; identifiable by title and through NCBFAA, ICPA and AAEI [RECALL as channels]. Two questions gate the whole experiment in the first interview: does the company classify in-house, or through vendor managed classification or a broker; and does the compliance lead ever issue a standing family rule that analysts must apply. A no to either ends the wedge before any log is coded.

## E. Competitive threat

Shape: a feature inside an existing workflow product, specifically the item-master and GTM owner. Second most likely: a consulting or service layer, since classification is already sold per line by brokers and as a managed service by Avalara (AVALARA, MCP README). "Not built at all" is the live outcome if the base-rate experiment returns single-digit H, which is the prior. Standalone survives only if H clears the economic gate at companies using several GTM systems or none, and the vendors decline to add the fields.

Reasoning. The unshipped piece is a field set and a link, not a system. Avalara already stores the settled code with user and date, an effective-dated executable override, dispute flags, a versioned automation mode, write-back and per-origin duty detail (AVALARA). ServiceNow and Marble have shipped the same increment in their own domains (SNOW, MARBLE). Every institution examined that runs a settlement loop built it itself (ATAR, CROSS, EBA, STRIPE for CE3.0). H5 makes any corpus single-tenant and H7 makes it exportable as tables, so a standalone vendor has no data moat and must win on process adoption and integrations, which the item-master owner already has. Who would absorb it: Avalara, Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE, Descartes, E2open, SAP GTS, Oracle GTM (feature sets unverified except Avalara); brokers and forwarder brokerage arms as a service layer; the authorities themselves on the public side (HMRC ships the loop as open source and is building AI drafting with review and provenance, per its repos); AWS AgentCore and Microsoft AGT if the product were framed as a generic control plane.

## G. Customer discovery design (10-interview sprint)

Target job titles. Director, Global Trade Compliance (importer or manufacturer); Customs Compliance Manager (importer); Senior Trade Compliance Analyst, Classification (importer); Licensed Customs Broker, Classification Manager (brokerage); Manager, Brokerage Compliance, at a global forwarder's brokerage arm; Trade Compliance Lead, Product Data, at a cross-border marketplace or retailer; Import Operations Manager at an industrial or MRO parts distributor; Product Manager, Global Trade or Classification, at a GTM vendor; customs and international trade attorney; Head of Customs at a UK or EU importer under ATaR or BTI.

Organisations. Two licensed brokerages (one regional with roughly 100 to 500 importer clients, one forwarder brokerage arm; named types, not commitments). One apparel or footwear importer as the control case where per-garment fibre and construction (category 3) should dominate; chapters 61 and 62 hold roughly 54,000 of about 260,000 CROSS documents (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OrlandoWhite88/qwen36-cpt-customs/main/README.md, open-source corpus). One consumer electronics importer (principal function). One industrial, automotive or MRO parts distributor (parts versus accessories, the richest expected source of categories 5, 7 and 8). One consumer goods company selling kits and sets (GIR 3(b)). One medical device maker classifying into several countries. One cross-border marketplace (tests automation by description-only classifiers such as HMRC's FPO model). One GTM vendor product team, interviewed early so H6 is answered before any coding. One trade law firm.

Questions, neutral form.
1. Walk me through the last new SKU or part that took more than one working day to classify. What was the hold-up: a missing spec, sample or vendor description, determining what the product is, or disagreement about what a rule means?
2. How many new items did your team classify in the last 12 months, into how many countries? How many went to a specialist or were escalated, and how does your system define escalated?
3. Do you classify in-house, through your GTM vendor's managed service, or through a broker? Where do escalations go in each case?
4. Where does a decision live once made: item master, spreadsheet, broker system, email? Does the record hold why the code was chosen, which facts were relied on, who approved it and which other items it applies to, or only code, user and date?
5. When a new product resembles something you classified before, how do you find the prior decision, and how often do you find it?
6. Tell me about the last time two people, or you and your broker, gave different codes to similar items. What happened next, and is there a log?
7. Does anyone in your company ever issue a standing position for a product family that analysts and brokers must apply? If so, in what form, and has the underlying question come back since?
8. In the last 12 months how many post-entry corrections, protests, reconciliation adjustments or refund claims traced to classification, and what were the stated reasons?
9. Have you requested a binding ruling in the last three years? For what fraction of your interpretive questions, and why not more often?
10. Who has authority to set a company classification position, and does it bind all entities and countries?
11. What does a classification cost you: internal hours per item and per escalation, broker per-line fees, counsel? Which budget line pays for tooling and who signs?
12. What does your GTM vendor already do for rationale, approval, applicability and version history? What have you asked for and what did they say?
13. How many people classify, and roughly how is their time split between gathering facts and deciding?
14. How often do HTS or CN changes invalidate a prior position, and how do you find affected items?

Evidence to request. Item-master export for 12 months with the Avalara-equivalent fields; the working classification worksheet with version history; escalation emails and broker requests, or a random 50 with attachments; broker instruction letters; the classification manual or family templates with versions; ruling requests and outcomes; the post-entry correction, protest and refund log with reasons; broker-importer disagreement records; team headcount and time allocation; broker fee schedule; GTM contract scope and price band; a sample of 20 escalated cases with attachments for pilot coding.

Answers that support. Escalations in the thousands a year with a material share described as arguments about rule meaning rather than facts. The same interpretive question returning within a year and being re-decided, or decided differently by brokers or other entities. Settled readings living in email, spreadsheets or heads, brokers uninstructed, the item master holding only code, user and date. Post-entry corrections traced by the interviewee to inconsistent application of something already decided. One named owner who ratifies family positions, binds brokers and entities, and would sign from a budget line that is not the GTM contract. The GTM product manager confirming no structured rationale, approval or applicability object exists or is planned. At least one company agreeing within two weeks to export the log under NDA.

Answers that kill. Most delay is spec sheets, samples and vendor descriptions, and most specialist time is fact gathering (H1). Escalations in the low hundreds a year at importers, with the broker pooled-queue path also dropped rather than used to rescue volume (H2, H8). Every item in a family is a fresh factual decision, so prior readings remove no work (category 3). Prior decisions are found through the item group and applied without re-deciding (H3). Settled readings already sit in ONESOURCE, Descartes, SAP GTS or Avalara with approval and version history (H3, H6). The company has no internal classifier because a broker or the vendor classifies per line (no buyer). Nobody writes family-general positions at all (category 8 impossible by practice). The owner resolves every interpretive question by requesting a ruling (H4). Tooling budget is the GTM contract and the buyer would take this only as a GTM or broker feature (H6). No company will share a log under NDA with a free audit in return.

Sequencing and recruiting. Week 1: the GTM vendor product manager and the two brokers, the latter for vocabulary, cost anchors and referrals, not as log sources. Weeks 2 to 3: the five importers through broker referrals, title search and association directories [RECALL as channels], including one UK or EU importer. Week 3: the marketplace. Week 4: the attorney last. Offer every company a free classification-consistency audit under NDA in exchange for the export. Do not recruit through vendor channels. Separate interviewers from coders so coders never hear what interviewees said about recurrence. Go or no-go after interview six: unless one in-house classifier has agreed to export a log, record the wedge as untestable. If no customs company exports, the named fallback is the same protocol on a bank's CDD exception log or a PSP's underwriting queue, so the stop result is "untestable here", not "untestable".

## H. Base-rate experiment

This is the reviewer-repaired design. Gates are fixed and pre-registered before coding and are not revised once shares are known.

Sample. 300 to 500 classification decisions per company from one to three companies, at least one an in-house importer team, from a single 12-month window, with a further 12 months of data for recurrence to avoid censoring. Two strata: (i) a random 150 to 200 from all decisions, to measure the all-decision share of categories 7 and 8; (ii) all escalated decisions if 300 or fewer, otherwise a random 200 to 300, stratified so no family exceeds 25%. If escalations cannot be identified, use a stated proxy and report it (completedAt minus createdAt over one working day; modifiedUserId differing from createdUserId; the vendor needs-review status), noting each is confounded and comparisons across companies with different proxies are invalid. Minimum stratum: about 100 identifiable escalations a year; below that H8 is answered by volume and the experiment is not run. Precision: at n = 400 and a true share of 15% the 95% interval is about plus or minus 3.5 points; at n = 200 per stratum about plus or minus 5 points, enough to separate 8% from 15% from 25%.

Source of cases. The GTM item-master export joined to the worksheet, escalation emails, broker requests and instruction letters, the classification manual with versions, ruling requests, and the correction and protest log. Per case the team assembles a decision packet: description and spec available at decision time, code per country, who and when, whether a prior same-family item existed at that date and what code it carried, whether a public ruling on the same line existed (full-text lookup needed; the CROSS CSV carries no ruling text, so use CBP's own search or the ATaR public search), escalation status and reason, later corrections, and the duty-rate differential between candidate codes. The packet records which elements of the HMRC Decision record (justification, methodSearch, methodCommercialDenomination, methodExclusion, effective dates, cancellation) the company record actually holds (ATAR), yielding a per-case record-sufficiency score that is a pre-registered secondary outcome.

Zero-coder metric, computed first from the export alone: items in the same item group or with near-identical descriptions carrying different codes for the same country; the same item coded differently across entities or brokers; items recoded after a correction. This is the first signal of capture failure and costs nothing.

Coding protocol. Two coders, both with tariff competence (a licensed broker or former classification specialist, and a former customs officer or trade counsel), independent of the supplying company and of each other, blind to the hypothesis and to the interviews. A third, non-specialist pass on a 60-case subsample tests whether the codebook carries the categories; it is descriptive, not a gate. Each coder assigns one primary and at most one secondary category plus a class key, through an ordered tree. Q1: was a schedule-required fact absent, causing a request, hold or delay? Yes: primary 2. Q2: with facts on file, could the coder reproduce the code in under five minutes from the notes and an existing family template or prior same-family decision, with no judgment call? Yes: primary 1. Q3: was the work determining what the product is under a rule whose meaning was not in dispute, such that another product in the family needs the same per-item work? Yes: primary 3. Q4: did the decision turn on what a rule text means? No: go to Q7. Yes: Q5: had the company settled that question for the same family before this date, evidenced by a citable dated record? Yes: primary 7 (record whether re-decided or contradicted). No: Q6: does the record show an authorised person stating the reading in family-general terms intended to govern future items? Yes: primary 8; if the reading exists only in this item's record: primary 5. Q7: did two authorities or instructions point to different codes and the decision resolved which prevails? Yes: primary 6. Q8: did the company choose among defensible codes on risk or commercial grounds for this shipment with no intent to govern other items? Yes: primary 4. Residue: 3, flagged. Because Q1 runs first, cases with both a facts request and an interpretive question fall to 2; the primary-or-secondary H share is therefore reported alongside the primary-only share, and the gate uses the primary-only share as pre-registered. Class key for 7 and 8: the 6-digit line or family times the interpretive question type. HMRC's reject reasons ATAR_RULING_ALREADY_EXISTS and DUPLICATE_APPLICATION (ATAR) are the coders' worked analogue of category 7. For broker records, the authorised person for category 8 is the client's compliance lead, never the broker.

Category definitions. (1) Fully deterministic: facts on file plus the GIRs, notes and an existing template or prior same-family decision yield one code with no judgment; a human touched it for entry or checking only; a prior settlement found and applied without re-deciding is coded 1 with its class key retained. (2) Missing evidence: blocked or delayed because composition, fibre content, function, material breakdown, sample or spec was absent. (3) Factual classification: facts available, but establishing essential character, principal function or material predominance required specialist reading under a rule whose meaning was not in question. (4) One-off discretion: a defensible code chosen on risk or commercial grounds for this shipment with no intent to govern other items. (5) Ambiguity of rule meaning left case-specific: the decision turned on rule text and the record states no family-level reading. (6) Conflict: two rulings, jurisdictions, instructions or templates pointed to different codes and the decision resolved which prevails. (7) Recurring previously-settled ambiguity: the question was settled by the company for the same family before this date, evidenced by a citable dated record, and a human re-decided it or decided inconsistently. (8) New ambiguity settled prospectively: the question was new and an authorised person stated the reading in family-general terms intended to govern future items.

Agreement. Pilot on 50 to 60 escalation-stratum cases; gate on the binary (7 or 8 versus other) at Cohen's kappa of at least 0.75, with the eight-way kappa reported descriptively. One codebook revision allowed, then a fresh pilot; a second failure stops the experiment and is reported as a finding that the records do not separate facts from interpretation, which is itself evidence against the product. Full sample: report percentage agreement, both kappas, each coder's shares and adjudicated shares. Adjudicator: the company's compliance lead, blind to which coder gave which code, who also states per category-8 case whether they would ratify the reading. Gates apply to the lower coder's pre-adjudication share; the trade-off is that thin records under-count category 7, which is why H is also reported conditional on the record-sufficiency score.

Thresholds, set by customer economics. All parameters are estimates until the interviews replace them, and the gates are recomputed with interview values before scoring. Model: L = N times C (escalations per year times loaded cost per escalation); S = h7 times r times L plus h8 times u times r times L minus capture cost, where h7 and h8 are the category shares, r is the fraction of an escalation's cost a settled reading removes, u is the expected within-year reuse rate of a new settlement, and capture cost is about one hour per settlement (estimate). Requirement: S at least 3P in year one, with P the annual price, because a mid-market buyer will not add a point tool beside a GTM suite on less than roughly three-to-one verifiable labour savings, and risk avoided is not verifiable at sale (heuristic). Central values: C 200 USD (range 100 to 300), N 2,500 (range 500 to 5,000), r 0.5, u 0.5, P 30,000 USD. Required h at the central case: 90,000 divided by (0.5 times 2,500 times 200) is 36%, above every H estimate in this project. At N 5,000 and C 300 it is 12%; at N 10,000 and C 300, 6%.

Gate 1, frequency: categories 7 plus 8 at least the required h computed from the interview-supplied L, which is 36% at the central estimates and 15% only where L reaches about 1.2 million USD; and at least 5% of the all-decision stratum. The 15% figure is also the payments verifier's kill line and is a tail value, not a default. Gate 2, recurrence: computed only on coded cases, each assigned an interpretive class, over the 24-month window; at least 50% of category 7 plus 8 cases fall in classes with three or more coded occurrences and no more than 40% of classes are singletons; line or family recurrence is reported separately and labelled factual recurrence. Basis: break-even is one reuse at r times C of 100 USD against 100 USD of capture cost, and compounding needs a median of three; the owner's own permission log (47.5% of settlements never reused, median two later calls, private data) is the prior for failure. Gate 3, capture failure: the re-decision rate among all cases where a prior settlement existed, 7 divided by (7 plus 1-with-class-key), at least 50%; if prior settlements were mostly found and applied, H3 holds. Gate 4, ratification: at least one third of category-8 cases ratified by the owner in family-general terms within the window. Gate 5, public duplication: fewer than 50% of category 7 plus 8 settlements match a public ruling on the same question at the decision date, judged on ruling text, not on line match, since 88 to 99% of decisions will match a prior ruling on the same line by construction (CROSS); if the gate fails, the remedy is retrieval over the public corpus, not a private ledger. Gate 6, records: no more than 20% of sampled cases score below the pre-registered record-sufficiency floor.

Shadow test. Four to eight weeks of prospective checking in which each new escalation is compared against the settlements captured from the retrospective sample, counting hits and whether the specialist would have applied the settlement. Retrospective coding measures recurrence and capture failure, not the effect of a tool; this is the cheapest first reading of that effect.

Decision rule. Continue toward a product only if all six gates pass at two or more companies, or at one company whose measured L times h clears the payback requirement with interview-supplied C and N, and the GTM vendor interview did not show the fields already exist. Pivot to a feature partnership or a broker-run service if h sits between 8% and the required gate, or if gates 1 to 4 pass in only one company, or if gate 5 fails. Stop the standalone product if h is below 8% everywhere, gate 2 or gate 3 fails everywhere, the pilot cannot reach the kappa gate after one revision, or no company exports a log within the sprint. Publish coder-level shares with intervals, gate values with the parameters used, and the list of category 7 and 8 classes with recurrence counts.

What it cannot decide. Willingness to pay, budget line, ACV or sales cycle. Whether ONESOURCE, Descartes, SAP GTS or Oracle GTM already carry the objects. Generalisation beyond the sampled companies (H5), whose willingness to share biases the sample in an unknown direction. Whether a captured settlement would be found at the moment of the next decision, beyond the shadow test. Cost per escalation and escalations per year, which are self-reported. Whether settled readings are right in law; a consistently applied wrong code compounds exposure, and a company position is a risk stance under reasonable-care obligations [RECALL]. Durability beyond the window. The boundary between categories 3 and 5 where the fact is itself a legal construct such as essential character; rule order treats it by convention. Market size or the number of companies above the L threshold.
