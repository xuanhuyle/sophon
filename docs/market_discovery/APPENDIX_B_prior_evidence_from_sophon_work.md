# Prior evidence from the Sophon investigation (github.com/xuanhuyle/sophon, 21 Sep 2026). PRIOR EVIDENCE TO BE TESTED, NOT AN ANCHOR.

The mechanism under test was already investigated once, on UK parliamentary expenses (IPSA). What that found, in case it helps you look in the right places or falsify faster:

- Expenses FAIL the economics: 997 paid / 1 not paid / 2 repaid in a 1,000-claim sample; full file 18 not paid and 446 repaid of 82,787; median published dispute £584. Checking before acting prevents almost nothing there. Limits plus after-the-fact audit is how the regulator actually controls 99.9% of claims.
- In 46 published IPSA appeal reviews: primary issue was discretionary relief or an exception in 18, meaning of a rule in 12, process advice 5, fact characterisation 4, evidence sufficiency 2, version timing 1. 22 to 24 of 46 stated a general reading that would decide future similar claims, BUT the reviewer cannot make it binding; 17 recommended a rule/guidance change; IPSA committed to 4. 16 of 46 outcomes relied on facts gathered after the original decision. "It was paid before" argued in 24 to 26, rejected in 15.
- Recurrence in 1,000 real claims: one settlement per class covers 93% at category+cost-type (67 classes) but only 37% at "what was actually bought" (633 classes, 86% seen once). Repetition is plentiful at the level policy owners already govern with bright lines and scarce where disputes arise.
- In the owner's own coding-agent permission history (9,404 tool calls): at the finest safe grant level, 906 settlements cover 90% of calls, 47.5% never reused, median 2 later calls, coverage flat across quarters.
- A 113-person study (Yan 2026, arXiv 2608.27443): people pre-authoring agent permission policies chose "ask me" for 114 of 140 rules.
- The investigation's own list of candidate workflows where checking before acting might pay (costly or irreversible actions, refusal rate not near zero, facts the system can observe itself): refunds and credits above a threshold, access grants, payment release, contract concessions, outbound data sharing. UNTESTED.
- Already shipped by incumbents: Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Policy (plain-English rule to Cedar, default-deny gateway); Ramp's policy agent (detected ambiguity becomes a suggested policy edit that a permitted admin publishes); Microsoft Agent Governance Toolkit; Auth0 async approvals; Permit.io one-time vs standing approvals. No product was found with typed, versioned interpretations and exceptions carrying who / when / what power, at ~65% confidence.
- Prior art: XACML administrative delegation, KeyNote, SPKI, macaroons, Biscuit, Catala / Rules-as-Code (pairs a lawyer with a programmer clause by clause).
- The investigation's standing conclusion: do not build a control plane; the only unshipped piece is a thin ledger of typed dated grants plus an escalation-to-settlement workflow; whether that is a product depends on whether escalation classes recur and whether policy owners settle them prospectively. That is exactly the question this market research must answer, in some domain other than expenses.
