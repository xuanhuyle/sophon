# Sophon — completed public IPSA follow-up test

**21 September 2026 | Scope: what public IPSA records can establish without an IPSA participant or internal case files**

## Decision

**The public-source IPSA test is complete at its useful evidence boundary. It does not validate or invalidate Sophon's product economics.** The claims export is unsuitable for scoring claim-level decisions, and published appeals are selected disputes, not a sample of ordinary decisions. The review corpus does, however, reveal consequential differences between a previous payment, a non-binding case remedy, a reviewer's reasoning, an IPSA policy amendment, and a later adjudication. It is valuable for testing provenance and temporal safeguards.

A sharper result than the earlier five-case study is that **previous practice can influence a review without authorizing a later claim**. The [17th Scheme §2.3](https://assets.ctfassets.net/nc7h1cs4q6ic/5j9dlpZsDtyFgqi43bBB91/7eaea5bcc329496878b420bf2e511586/Seventeenth_edition_of_the_Scheme_2024-25.pdf) states that an individual determination does not bind IPSA on subsequent claims of the same nature. Yet the [Pound briefcase review](https://assets.ctfassets.net/w59ykwiqehvh/4fvx1BfzRCc4x1uxMDIKZt/656374c37746b3bc54eea5217e2b7c5e/Stephen_Pound_____Statement_of_Findings.pdf), paragraphs 32–43 and the CEO response, explicitly considers earlier paid laptop-bag claims as a consistency concern. Treating previous payments as irrelevant would miss useful evidence; converting them directly into future payment permission would exceed the source.

## Test actually performed

1. Rechecked the official [completed-reviews index](https://www.ipsacompliance.org.uk/investigations/completed-reviews) and verified SHA-256 hashes for **all 46 linked PDFs** against the frozen inventory. Forty-five had usable extracted text; the First-tier Tribunal decision initially yielded only 13 characters, so I OCRed all 13 pages, obtaining 34,598 characters. The corpus comprises **42 review/finding documents, two explicitly provisional reports, one brief internally resolved summary, and one Tribunal decision**. Those are document types, not independent claim counts.
2. Scanned all 46 documents for language about prior practice, precedent, later evidence, future guidance and Scheme change; inspected document openings and decisions; then manually checked **13 consequential documents** against their original reports and recorded 22 text anchors. The 13 were chosen to stress the thesis after seeing corpus signals; this is a **post hoc case series**, not a blind sample or a precision/recall estimate. The source-audit program reproduces the document counts and phrase hits, and the case-check program verifies that the recorded anchors exist. They do not validate the analyst's legal classifications.
3. Retained the earlier **1,000-row label-blind public claims replay** as an observability check: 1,000 `REQUEST_FACT`, zero `CLEAR`, zero `BLOCK`; 997 published Paid, one Not Paid, two Repaid after the freeze. It used the 15th Scheme across all of 2023–24 even though a [contemporary IPSA review](https://assets.ctfassets.net/w59ykwiqehvh/18t8MeQrTBBsNGJQoqpVs9/9eccd2744f3ce9e835f042aad08f968a/20231020-IPSA-COM-1491-KAWCZYNSKI-Review-Final.pdf) identifies the revised 16th edition as applicable in July 2023. **No historical-policy accuracy claim is drawn from that replay.** Its missing submission evidence, certification, budget state and internal approvals remain an observable data limitation. [IPSA's publication page](https://www.theipsa.org.uk/mp-staffing-business-costs/annual-publications) states that some information is withheld and the export is periodically updated.

The all-document keyword scan found `precedent`/`precedence` language in eight PDFs, prior/similar practice terms in 21, future/change terms in 17, and later-evidence terms in 21. These counts locate passages. They **do not** count binding precedents, enacted amendments, successful interpretations or factual leakage; claimant submissions, reviewer text and attachments can each trigger a hit.

## What this test decides

**Confirmed about the public data:** We can reproduce the field-observability failure in the 2023–24 export, audit the provenance of all 46 published documents, and find real cases that distinguish previous practice, written rule, proposal, review outcome and legally relevant time. The previous five-case guard passed six narrow tests; this follow-up adds 13 source-checked stress cases, including eight not in that original five-case set. Its evidence-check code confirms source traceability, **not** a deployed engine's correctness.

**Rejected as a test design:** A public IPSA historical replay cannot measure Sophon's autonomous clearance rate, false-clear rate, time saved by ratified interpretations, or amendment maintenance against a strong conventional policy workflow. The export lacks claim-time supporting documents and ledger state; the appeal reports select exceptional disputes and often use records or testimony collected after the initial decision. Published Paid/Not Paid/Repaid statuses are not independent normative labels for a prospective action. Scheme editions changed during the year tested. More case coding alone cannot fill those missing measurements.

**Product verdict:** Undetermined. These examples specify adversarial requirements that any candidate architecture should confront, but they do not choose an architecture. In particular, the evidence does not establish the need for a new normative control plane over a competent existing rules/workflow system. A fair product test requires one bounded workflow with decision-time case files, versioned policy and guidance, accountable owner decisions, actual review effort, and a comparison with a capable simpler approach. That may be a different domain; this report should not constrain Claude Code's choice.

## Reproduction and limits

The companion packet contains `sophon_cases/corpus_inventory.json` with 46 official URLs and frozen PDF hashes; `sophon_ipsa_followup/prepare_corpus.py` obtains and verifies those PDFs, extracts text with `pdftotext`, and OCRs the Tribunal PDF with `pdftoppm` and `tesseract`. `audit.py` writes the document-wide navigation inventory and counts; `verify_checks.py` checks the 13 manually coded observations against 22 short anchors in the original text. All 46 hash checks, the 13 case checks, and the original 2 replay plus 6 authority-probe guard tests passed in this run. Re-running the primary claims replay against a historically corrected Scheme was **not** attempted: source applicability and decision-time facts would first need to be established, and all 1,000 original abstentions supplied no claim accuracy signal. No IPSA policy owner or affected person was contacted; no reimbursement was made.
